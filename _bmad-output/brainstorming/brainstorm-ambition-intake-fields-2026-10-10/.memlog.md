---
topic: What the ambition (goal intake) section should capture beyond one line of text
goal: Give the AI enough real context to generate quality missions instead of guessing deadline, level, budget and resources
mode: partner
updated: 2026-10-12T11:09
---

- (insight) Baseline: the form shows one text box. API also accepts weekly time budget, location, timezone but the form never asks. AI guesses deadline, level, resources; defaults time budget to 180 min/week silently.
- (direction by user) Techniques chosen: Yes And Building, Reverse Brainstorming, Assumption Reversal, Laddering
- (technique) started Yes And Building
- (idea by coach) A 'where are you starting from' field: your current level against the goal (today's 5km time, current lift, savings so far) so the plan starts at your real position
- (idea by user) Goals differ in the kind of finish line: qualitative (get into med school) vs quantitative (bench 100kg), so the intake should know which it is
- (idea by coach) Branch the form on finish-line type: quantitative asks for current number + target number + unit (72kg to 100kg); qualitative asks 'how will you know you've made it?' in your own words (an acceptance letter from any Australian med school)
- (idea by user) Deadlines differ per person and per goal, so the intake needs the person's own deadline
- (idea by coach) Deadline has a kind, not just a date: hard and set by someone else (UCAT sitting, application close, race day: plan backwards, can't move), soft and self-chosen ('by summer': AI may propose a more realistic date), or none (habits). If start + weekly time can't reach a hard deadline, say so honestly rather than squeeze the plan
- (idea by user) Each goal gets a different number of hours per week, so ask for the weekly time budget per goal
- (insight) Cheap win: the API already accepts weeklyTimeBudgetMinutes; only the form is missing it. Today the AI defaults to 180 min/week when unstated
- (idea by coach) Ask for the shape of the time, not just the total: one long block vs short daily sessions (3h Saturday suits long runs, 20 min daily suits a language), plus time of day / energy. And account for time shared across several missions, since a person can now hold more than one
- (idea by user) The AI should know the user's current benchmark
- (idea by coach) A benchmark for qualitative goals too: for 'get into med school' it's predicted ATAR, a UCAT practice score, work experience done. Plus how it was measured (tested vs guessed), so the AI knows how far to trust it
- (idea by coach) Past attempts as a benchmark: 'have you tried this before, and where did it stall?' (quit running at week 3, shin splints). The plan can design around the exact point it failed last time
- (idea by user) The AI needs to know what resources the person has
- (idea by user) Concrete case: wants a 99+ ATAR but can only afford one tutor, so money limits paid help to one subject and the plan has to work within that
- (idea by coach) Scarce-resource allocation: when something is limited (one tutor), the AI decides where it goes for the most leverage and covers the rest with self-study, and says why
- (idea by coach) A generic 'parts of the goal and where you stand on each' field instead of an ATAR-specific one: subjects for ATAR, swim/bike/run for a triathlon, product/sales/marketing for a business. Keeps it a general life primitive (law 7: not an exam tracker)
- (idea by coach) An explicit budget for the goal, so the AI never prescribes a course, coach or equipment the person can't afford
- (idea by user) Family commitments are a limit the plan has to work around
- (idea by coach) Ask how predictable your life is, not just what commitments you have. Predictable (school pickup 3pm weekdays) means plan around the blocked times. Unpredictable (caring for a sick parent) means interruption-proof plans: short steps, catch-up slack, nothing that collapses if you miss a day, and no shame for missing one (law 8)
- (idea by user) Personal ability is a limit the plan has to work around
- (idea by coach) Ability should shape the plan's format, not just its content: an injury changes which exercises, ADHD might mean shorter blocks with more variety, dyslexia more audio or video. Health details are sensitive: optional, explained, deletable, and they should trigger the existing safety rules (point to a physio) rather than the AI improvising medical advice
- (idea by coach) Ask about strengths as well as limits: 'what are you already good at?' lets the plan lean on them, e.g. a strong maths student uses it to carry harder subjects
- (direction by user) Moved on from Yes And Building to the next technique
- (technique) started Reverse Brainstorming
- (idea by coach) Failure mode: make the form 40 required questions before you see any mission, so people quit before they ever get value
- (idea by user) Failure mode: ask about constraints in a demeaning way, e.g. making 'not enough money' feel like a personal failing
- (idea by coach) Inverse: ask what you CAN put toward the goal ('what can you spend on this?' with ranges including 'nothing, free only') rather than what you lack. Every constraint question is skippable and nothing hints the goal is out of reach because of it (law 8)
- (idea by coach) Failure mode: the AI comments on the person, e.g. 'with your budget a 99 ATAR is unlikely'. Inverse: the AI may say a PLAN is too tight and offer a better one, but never judges the PERSON or their circumstances
